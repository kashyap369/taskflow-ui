## Start here

TaskFlow has two kinds of account, and they behave differently.

**Individual** gives you a personal workspace: your own tasks, your own progress, and an inbox for invitations from organizations.

**Organization** gives you a shared workspace you own: members, roles, teams, projects, meetings and reports.

You are not locked in either direction. An Individual account can be invited into any number of organizations, and an Organization account can be invited into someone else's workspace too.

## The six things, in order

Everything in an organization builds on the thing before it. Setting up in this order saves going back to fix access later.

1. **Organization** - the workspace itself. Whoever creates it is the owner.
2. **Roles** - what a kind of member may do. Every permission is granted here.
3. **Members** - people who accepted an invitation. Each one holds a role.
4. **Teams** - groups of members, for organising and reporting.
5. **Projects** - containers for related work.
6. **Tasks and subtasks** - the work itself, assigned to one member each.

> The single most common misunderstanding: **teams do not grant permissions**. A person's access comes entirely from their role. Teams are for organising and reporting.

## What reads from all of this

Calendar, Meetings and Reports mostly store nothing of their own - they are views over the six things above. That explains their two most common surprises:

- A task with **no due date** never appears on the calendar.
- Work with **no logged time** shows as no work in the time reports.

## Getting help later

Every page has a **Help** button in the bottom-right corner. It explains that page, offers a step-by-step tour of it, and links to the pages related to it. Nothing here is one-time - tours can be replayed whenever you want.
