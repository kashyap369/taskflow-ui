## What a task is

The unit of work in TaskFlow. A task has:

- a **project** it belongs to
- one **assignee** - a member of the organization
- a **status** - what stage it is at
- a **due date** - optional, but needed for the calendar and for overdue tracking

Almost every other screen is a view over these.

## Assignees

The assignee list is drawn from your organization's members. Somebody has to accept an invitation and become a member before they can be given work - a pending invitation is not enough.

## Subtasks

Subtasks break one task into checkable steps. They are deliberately simple: **subtasks are not separately assigned or dated**. If a piece of work needs its own owner or its own deadline, it should be a task, not a subtask.

Use subtasks to show progress inside a task. Use tasks to divide work between people.

## Time tracking

Log hours against a task as the work happens, or add them manually afterwards. The history shows what has been logged and by whom.

This matters more than it looks: **every time report is built entirely from these entries.** Work that is done but never logged shows up as no work at all.

## Filtering

Narrow the list by project, status or assignee to answer a specific question - what a team is doing this week, what one person has open, what is stuck - without leaving the page.

## Statuses

Moving a task's status is what drives the dashboard, the reports and the completion trend. A team that finishes work but never marks it done will look, everywhere else in TaskFlow, like a team that finished nothing.
